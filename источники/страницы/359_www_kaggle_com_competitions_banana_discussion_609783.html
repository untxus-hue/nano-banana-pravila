Winners of the Nano Banana Hackathon!

First, let me say what an incredible job all participants have done. There was tremendous creativity and insight put into the submissions, ranging far and wide in terms of innovative applications and complex techniques.

As we shared when Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Nano Banana) was released, multimodal capability and visual consistency are foundational to Google DeepMind’s approach to generative models. This challenge was part of our commitment to advancing innovative, beneficial AI and raising the standard of creative applications across the ecosystem. This competition truly showed the Kaggle community at its best and we couldn't be happier with the ingenuity displayed.

How Was Judging Done?

The challenge itself saw huge levels of engagement, resulting in over 800 total submissions—making this one of Kaggle’s largest hackathons ever!

Our judging process was thorough and intense:

1) Initial Screening: We first conducted a highly objective filter based on essential criteria: the mandatory use of the Gemini 2.5 Flash Image API, the presence of a video demo, and a live, publicly accessible demo URL (not just a GitHub link or notebook). This ensured we advanced only high-quality, functional projects. 2) Deep Expert Review: The second round consisted of a deep review of the top 489 qualified submissions by our expert judges. This included making every effort to validate and replicate the reported results and to access all linked artifacts. Judging was based on four core criteria: Innovation/Wow Factor (40%), Technical Execution (30%), Potential Impact (20%), and Presentation Quality (10%). 3) Final Deliberation: Finally, all top-scoring submissions were discussed in depth by the judges. These discussions were both rigorous and rewarding because the overall quality of innovation, especially around consistency and fusion, was so high.

We are confident that this rigorous process selected the most outstanding and deserving projects that truly showcase the transformative power of Nano Banana.

Key Developer Insights and Technical Themes

Overall, the judges were incredibly impressed by the solutions demonstrated. The common themes proved that developers understood the core value proposition of Gemini 2.5 Flash Image. Here are a few of the most interesting technical findings:

1) Consistency Solved: The majority of top submissions proved that the Character Consistency problem, a long-standing hurdle in generative media, is effectively solved using the Gemini 2.5 Flash Image model, opening the door for production-ready storytelling and e-commerce applications. 2) Complex Fusion Mastery: We saw numerous projects successfully execute complex Multi-Image Fusion by blending 3–5 distinct inputs (e.g., character photo, scene reference, style guide, and product image) into a single, cohesive output. 3) Intelligent Refinement: The most innovative projects leveraged the model's capacity for Iterative Editing, using the previous generated image as a critical input for the next generation. This technique allowed for sophisticated conversational editing (e.g., "Make it sunset," "Move object closer") that preserves original image DNA. 4) Beyond Art: A significant portion of winning projects applied the multimodal capabilities to technical domains, such as Scientific Illustration, CAD Conversion, and Medical Visualization, proving the utility of the AI model far beyond basic image generation. 5) Prompt Orchestration: Many submissions used sophisticated multi-model pipelines (e.g., Gemini Flash for structured JSON scripts, then Nano Banana for image creation, and ElevenLabs for voice-over), showcasing a mastery of AI orchestration.

Why So Many Submissions Were Strong (and What Set the Winners Apart) With over 800 submissions, this hackathon set a new bar for creativity and engagement. Everyone who reached the qualified judging round should be incredibly proud of their work. We know how much time and passion you invested, and the high quality showed across the board.

While we can't provide individualized feedback for every submission that was not selected as a winner, we wanted to share a high-level observation to recognize the outstanding effort and provide context for future challenges.

Across the field, submissions were particularly strong in two areas: Innovation/Wow Factor and Technical Execution. We saw brilliant, original ideas and complex, well-executed code from countless participants. However, in the final deep-dive review, the projects that ultimately earned a spot demonstrated a slight edge in two other key areas that often separated the very best from the simply excellent:

1) Presentation Quality: This isn't just about a flashy video; it's about clarity and persuasion. The winning submissions delivered a polished, easy-to-understand narrative. Their videos and live demos quickly and convincingly showed the judges exactly what the project did and how it solved a critical user pain point using the new model.

2) Potential Impact: The winners often clearly articulated who would use this product and why it matters to them. They moved beyond a cool demo to show a clear path to solving a real-world problem—whether in e-commerce, medicine, or education—that could deliver lasting, tangible value.

Essentially, while many submissions were dazzling on the what and how, the winning projects also mastered the so what and for whom.

Congratulations again to all winners and to the entire community for a fantastic competition! We look forward to seeing what you build next.
